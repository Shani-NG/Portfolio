**PROFILE OVERVIEW**

Shani Nakash-Gomel is a senior UX strategist and innovation lead with more than a decade of experience in complex systems across defense, medical, learning, and enterprise environments. Her work is characterized by analytical thinking, design-method discipline, cross-functional translation, and a strong preference for meaningful work with visible impact.

She combines strategic perspective with practical execution. She is comfortable shaping vision, structure, and process, while also going into detailed interaction logic, design-system decisions, accessibility, and implementation constraints when needed.

**WORKING APPROACH**

**Problem solving**

Shani does not usually commit to the first plausible solution. She explores several directions, compares metaphors, structures, and interaction models, and converges only after understanding what each option reveals.

This pattern should be interpreted as methodical exploration rather than indecision. Iteration is used to reduce risk, expose weak assumptions, and increase confidence in the final direction.

Complexity ownership

A recurring question in her work is: who should carry the complexity—the user or the system?

She actively evaluates when complexity should be hidden, when transparency is necessary, and when operational clarity matters more than aesthetic simplicity.

Evidence before confidence

She prefers grounded decisions, explicit constraints, and traceable reasoning. Unsupported claims, vague praise, or decorative complexity are generally rejected.

MVP discipline

She values quality and precision, but recognizes the need to release “good enough” versions earlier when speed and learning matter. Managing the balance between depth and pace remains an active development area.

**COLLABORATION AND LEADERSHIP**

**Leadership through alignment**

Shani typically mobilizes people by identifying their actual needs and pain points rather than relying on formal authority.

Equal-footing partnership

In cross-functional teams, she prefers to operate as an active, influential partner rather than from a detached or hierarchical position.

Bridge between disciplines

She translates human, experiential, and business needs into structured language that developers, architects, product teams, and managers can act on—and translates technical constraints back into user and product implications.

Communication

Her preferred style is direct, thoughtful, and specific. She looks for useful feedback rather than reassurance, and she is willing to adjust when evidence or professional reasoning supports a change.

Assertiveness

Empathy and diplomacy are strengths. In situations where alignment cannot be achieved through soft influence alone, more direct and explicit decision-making may be required.
